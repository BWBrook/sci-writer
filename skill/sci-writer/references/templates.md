# Templates

## The handover pack (working agents)

A folder the lead author can work from without reading the repository.

- `agent-guide.md`: what the study established, with claim IDs, the vocabulary of the files, and
  the known misreadings.
- `claims.csv`: each claim with its ID, status, value and limit. Where a claim says "every" or
  "all", it says every what: replicate, scenario or site. The ledger must hold everything the
  article may state; a statement in an old draft that the ledger lacks is added with its evidence
  or dropped.
- `estimands.md`: what each analysis bounds, and the phrasings that are not allowed.
- `figures.md` and the figure files: the candidate display items with captions, and for each
  figure a line saying which rules, groups or scenarios it actually shows.
- `tables-and-supplement.md`: an index of the tables and supplement sections, so that the writer
  can say where a value left out of the prose is to be found.
- `methods.md`: the methods as facts.
- `venue.md`: the target journal's requirements, and what five recent papers there have in common:
  sections, length, display items, how the abstract and first paragraph are built.
- `literature-map.md`: what the field already knows; what was known about the mechanism; what this
  study adds; what each reference is cited for.
- `our-account.md`: a few plain paragraphs from the working agents. Which three results matter
  most? What did you expect? What surprised you? What do you think it means? What would you say to a
  colleague in the corridor? Record expectations when the work is planned; they cannot be
  recovered afterwards.

## The plan (lead author, stage 2)

The working abstract is a planning aid. If the seven answers come easily, keep them short and move
on; the main message is the part that must be right.

```
READERS (article)
1. <who they are> — knows: ... — does not know: ...
2. ...
READERS (brief)
1. ...
MAIN MESSAGE (25 words or fewer, positive)
...
WORKING ABSTRACT (about 25 words each)
Why is this paper needed?
What is the aim or question?
What did we expect, and on what grounds?
What did we do?
What did we find?
What do the results mean?
What is it good for, and how far does it generalise?
DISPLAY ITEMS (budget N)
1. <item> — the claim it establishes
CUT LIST (must not be empty)
<claim ID> — why it stays out — where it goes
VOCABULARY
Readers already own: ...
We must teach (term — the plain sentence that introduces it): ...
Stays out of the prose (project term — what we say instead): ...
Quantities (name — what is counted — against what): ...
```

## The outline (stage 4)

Built from the one-page paper: each place where it asks the reader to take a step on trust becomes
one or more paragraphs.

```
SECTION <name> — limit <n> words
P1 [<target words>] Reader's question: ... | Point: ... | claims: C.. | display: Fig. n |
   numbers allowed: a, b | condition (only if the point misleads without it): ...
P2 ...
LIMITATIONS PASSAGE [<target words>] — the qualifications that go here: ...
LEFT IN TABLES OR SUPPLEMENT: ...
```

Read the points in order. They should make the argument, and each reader's question should arise
from the point before it.
