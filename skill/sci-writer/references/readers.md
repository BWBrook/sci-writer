# Instructions for readers

Each reader works from the text alone. Do not give a cold reader the plan, the repository or these
rules. A reader should come from a different lab than the writer where possible.

Readers report; the lead author decides the wording. A model's read locates faults. It does not
show that a person would follow or enjoy the text, and it should not be used to rank drafts.

## Cold reader

Give the reader a description of who they are (taken from the plan's readers) and the text. Ask:

1. What question does this text set out to answer?
2. What is its main finding, in one or two sentences?
3. What condition or limit most affects how that finding should be taken?
4. Does the end answer the question the start raised?
5. Where did you lose the thread, and where did you want to stop reading? Quote the place.
6. Which terms or quantities did you have to guess at? Quote each.
7. If it had to be a third shorter, what would you cut?

Do not ask for a list of every unclear phrase: a reader asked for that returns about twenty
whatever the text.

Compare answers 1 to 3 with the plan. The comparison is the result.

## Reader's editor

Give the editor the text and the description of its readers. Ask it to mark, with quotations:

- where the argument is hard to follow from one sentence or paragraph to the next;
- where the text is flat: a paragraph with no point, or a point with nothing to picture;
- where it tries too hard: a device from the list in `prose.md` used without a reason;
- where one thing has two names, or a quantity appears before it is introduced;
- which paragraph it would cut first.

It may suggest wording for up to five places. It does not rewrite the text.

## Truth-checker (a working agent)

Give the checker the draft, the claims ledger and the interpretation specification. For every
sentence that states a result:

1. Find the claim it rests on. Report any number that is not in the ledger.
2. Compare the sentence with the claim on five points: what is asserted; how certain it is said to
   be; how it relates to the evidence (observed, modelled, inferred); what role it plays in the
   argument; and its qualifications and scope.
3. Search for each phrasing the interpretation specification does not allow.

Report each fault as: the sentence, the claim ID, what differs, and how serious it is (wrong;
misleading to the described reader; or only less cautious than the ledger). Do not reword. Do not
ask for a qualification to be restored unless its absence makes the sentence wrong or misleading.

## Co-author from another lab (before any prose)

Given the pack only, write your own main message (25 words or fewer) and your own one-page paper
(about 600 words). Do not look at the lead author's. Say which result you would lead with and which
you would leave out.
