# Writing the prose

For the lead author at stage 5, and for anyone editing. Marks as in `SKILL.md`. Barry's own words
and their dates are in `docs/decisions.md` of `BWBrook/sci-writer`.

## The whole text

- One question and one main message. A reader could repeat the argument to a colleague **[B]**.
- Give the answer early and return to it at the close **[P; S: Brown, Pressland and Rogers; Pinker]**.
  The message appears in the title, the abstract, the opening of the discussion and the close, in
  different words each time, and the text never remarks on its own repetition.
- An article is an argument and the supplement holds the inventory **[B]**. What is left out is
  still on record in the ledger and the guide for agents.
- State a view: what was expected, what was surprising, what the authors think it means **[B]**.
  Enthusiasm shows in what you choose to tell the reader. Adjectives do not carry it.

## Paragraphs

- One job each. Open with the point and stop when the job is done **[B]**.
- The first words connect to what the reader has just read **[S: Gopen and Swan]**.
- Point first, condition second, number last **[B]**.
- One or two numbers in a paragraph, rounded unless the precision matters. Exact values belong in
  tables **[B]**.
- The example before the rule, and the concrete thing before the abstract one **[B]**. Say what the
  object looks like and what was counted before interpreting it. One running example, returned to
  when the argument turns technical, serves better than several **[S: Pierce]**.

## Sentences

- Keep the subject close to its verb, and let the verb name the action **[S: Gopen and Swan]**.
- Begin with what the reader already has. Put the new and important thing where the sentence
  closes **[S: Gopen and Swan; Pinker]**.
- One main claim to a sentence. A condition that governs the claim stays with it; a second claim
  gets its own sentence.
- Say who did what. "We counted" is plainer than "counts were obtained" **[S: Whitesides; Maddox,
  quoted by Brown]**. A passive is right when it keeps the topic first.
- Complete every comparison: higher than what, less often than when **[S: Whitesides]**.
- Follow "this" with its noun **[S: Whitesides]**.
- State findings positively **[B]**. Deny something only when the reader is likely to believe it.

## Words

- One name for each thing, held from the abstract to the last table **[B]**.
- Ordinary words. A term the readers already own needs no definition; a term they lack is taught
  once, in a plain sentence, where it first appears; a project's private label stays out **[B, S:
  Pinker]**.
- The literal phrase where one exists. An analogy earns its place only by explaining what the
  literal phrase cannot, and you should be able to say where it stops applying **[B, S]**.
- Introduce every quantity before using it: what is counted, and against what **[B]**.
- Replace a hedge with the condition or with the size of the uncertainty, where known **[S:
  Pinker]**. Do not delete a hedge that carries the scope of a claim; the truth check decides
  **[S: Centonze]**.

## Qualifications

- A qualification that changes how a reader should take a headline finding sits beside that
  finding, once **[B, P]**.
- All others go to one short limitations passage, with the detail in a supplement table **[B]**.
  That passage has a word limit set in the outline; Barry found that agents let it swell.

## Devices that AI writers overuse

None is banned; each needs a reason at the place it is used, and a second use on a page needs a
better one **[B]**. The full list with examples is `docs/overused-devices.md`.

Denial before the claim; announcing importance ("what matters is"); an abstraction as the actor
("the record holds"); a pointer before the thing it points to; a heading that teases; a maxim at the
end of a paragraph; a wrong reading raised to be dismissed; a run of fragments; a metaphor where a
literal phrase exists; a bold label before a sentence. Words to watch: "quiet", "silence" for an
absence, the suffix "-shaped".

## A test for any sentence

Could you read it aloud to a colleague without it sounding like a performance? **[P; S: Brown,
Pressland and Rogers, "write it as you would say it"]**

## The brief

Two A4 pages for anyone who finds the repository: a journalist, a student, a scientist from another
field **[B]**. The main finding comes first. Assume no technical term. Teach at most a handful, and
prefer dropping an idea to defining another term **[P]**. Few findings, each made concrete; the rest
is in the article. No claim IDs in the text the reader sees.

## How these rules go wrong when obeyed to the letter

- Meeting a word limit by packing clauses.
- Defining terms every reader of the journal knows.
- Cutting a hedge that was the claim's scope.
- Treating a count from the script as a score.
- Removing every instance of a device and leaving the prose flat.
- Moving all qualifications into a limitations passage that then runs to a page.
