# Barry's methods for writing a paper

Four documents by Barry W. Brook, the second with Jessie Buettel, supplied on 19 November 2026 and
committed with their agreement (Barry: "Jessie gives her imprimatur. It's meant for general
helpfulness."). They are the starting point for the process this repository will build. They are
reproduced as written and have not been edited to this repository's style.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `1-structured-method-original.md` | The original eleven-step method for students. Main message in 25 words, working abstract, title, display items, a skeleton with each paragraph's message in 15 words, then the paragraphs. |
| `2-structural-planning-2023.md` | Brook and Buettel, revised 2023. A cover page of answers, a "bag of bones", a skeleton that co-authors agree, then prose that maps exactly to the skeleton. |
| `3-expanding-abstract-for-students.md` | The expanding abstract. A working abstract, the whole paper in one page of about 600 words, four section abstracts of 300 words, paragraph abstracts of three or four sentences, then the full draft. |
| `4-expanding-abstract-agent-prompts.md` | The prompts Barry pasted to an agent, in order, to rebuild an existing draft by the expanding-abstract steps. It adds steps for agents: who cares about the result, the target journal, a comparison with five recent papers from that journal, and a budget for display items. |

All four build on Brown, Pressland and Rogers (1993), "Righting scientific writing: focus on your
main message!", *The Rangeland Journal* 15(2): 183–189, doi:10.1071/rj9930183. That paper is
copyrighted and is held only in the local `library/`.

`originals/` holds the two `.docx` files as supplied. The PDF of the original method stays in the
local `library/barry/`, because its file metadata names a third party.

## Where the agent version fell short

Barry said of the agent prompts that they "kind of worked" (19 November 2026). Fable proposed that
the shortfall was at the last expansion: the compressed stages came out well, and the full draft
then brought back the numbers and the caveats. Barry: "Yes, good diagnosis." On that account the
method repairs the argument and leaves the prose to be repaired separately.
