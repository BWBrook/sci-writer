# Astra on the first draft of the skill

Astra (OpenAI `gpt-6.2-astra`, maximum effort), 19 November 2026. Fable sent it the four files of skill draft 0.1 inline and asked what a model would ignore, what it would obey too literally, and what to change. Reproduced as returned. Draft 0.2 adopted most of it; see `docs/process.md`.

---

I’d keep the fresh writer and the one-page paper. I think the trouble starts in stage 4. The outline records each paragraph’s claim, figure and numbers. It drops the reason the reader needs this paragraph after the previous one. A model can follow that outline faithfully and turn a readable one-page paper into a 4,000-word inventory.

**What I’d quietly ignore.** I’d fill in the reader profiles, then write for a generic scientist. Once I’d included a sensitivity analysis, I could always say Reader 2 needed it to “believe” the finding. The expansion audit gives me a ready justification for every addition. The read-aloud test is similarly easy to pass on my own judgment.

**What I’d obey too literally.** Expect numbers in paragraphs that need none, an estimate separated from its interval to meet the allowance, and four paraphrases of the headline. “State findings positively” carries a scientific risk. “We could not distinguish the two treatments” can become “the treatments worked equally well.”

**Four changes I’d make**

1. **Use the one-page paper as the start of the draft.** Replace the section abstracts with this instruction. “Mark each place where the one-page paper asks the reader to take a step on trust. Expand there. State the question still open and identify the observation, comparison or design detail that answers it. Stop once the reader can follow the inference.” For example, if winter detections fell and the cameras ran for fewer nights, the next question is whether visits also fell *per working camera-night*. That gives the effort correction a reason to appear.

2. **Replace the expansion audit with one worked example and an actual edit.** Give the writer an outline sentence from another study, the facts available, the paragraph Barry would want to read, and one accurate detail left in the figure. At review, ask the editor to shorten the first passage where excess detail obscures the point. The writer can then judge a specific cut. I’d also drop the seven-part working abstract and the provenance marks from the writer’s copy, and put any venue requirements in the pack.

3. **Settle the conflict over qualifications.** Stage 6 sends requests for caution to the limitations passage, even when omitting a condition makes the claim misleading. Use this wording. “Keep the comparison and any uncertainty needed to interpret it together. State a condition beside the finding wherever that finding must stand alone. If a secondary finding takes too long to state accurately, move the finding and its condition together.” Run the truth check on the final abstract, legends and brief. All three are written after the current check.

4. **Ask the cold reader to reconstruct the reasoning.** A reader can recover the question, headline and main condition from the abstract alone. Ask instead, “Starting with what the authors measured, how did they reach their conclusion? Where did you have to supply a step yourself?” For the trial, I’d ask Barry to mark the first passage he had to reread to understand what the authors meant. That gives the group an exact place to examine even if every model reviewer passes the draft.
