### Unclear or loose wording

1. **"Clocks"** — Without a background in evolutionary biology, it is unclear what kind of dating method this refers to (for instance, genetic clocks versus geological dating) or why different clocks disagree.
2. **"how missing fossils are read"** — The phrasing is loose because it does not explain what it means to interpret an absence of evidence, such as treating empty intervals as proof of absence versus estimating the odds that fossils were missed.
3. **"how much the missing fossils can bear"** — This metaphor is opaque, leaving unclear whether "bear" means how much evidentiary weight the gap carries, how long a hidden history it can rule out, or how much uncertainty it allows.
4. **"The silence before"** — It is not stated how far back in time this fossil-free span is measured, such as across the disputed 60-million-year window or further back.
5. **"vascular-plant"** — The term is undefined for a general reader, and the text shifts from "living land plants" to "vascular plants" without explaining whether they are the same group or one is a subset of the other.
6. **"is worth about three finds, which is very little"** — It is unclear how an absence of fossils converts into a count of "three finds" (presumably the number of discoveries one would have expected if plants were present) or by what standard three counts as "very little".
7. **"were found several times less often"** — This omits both the comparison baseline (less often than what) and whether "found" refers to how readily early plants fossilised, how well their rocks survived, or how thoroughly palaeontologists have searched.
8. **"one long quiet stretch of that kind"** — It is unclear whether this means a later period when plants are known to have existed yet fossils are similarly scarce, and how long a span counts as "long".
9. **"the late Silurian"** — A reader without a geological timescale in mind does not know when this period occurred, how long after the first fossils it falls, or why reaching it counts as the quiet ending "fast".
10. **"close to their full rate"** — It is unclear what a "full rate" of fossils is (such as a typical later discovery rate per million years or a maximum preservation rate) and whether "their" refers to vascular plants or all land plants.
11. **"something we rarely find and then became findable quickly"** — "Findable" is loose because it blurs whether the plants themselves evolved traits that fossilise more easily, whether geological preservation improved, or whether the rocks were better sampled.

### What the paragraph claims

The authors calculated that the lack of fossils before the earliest known vascular plants is weak evidence against an older origin—roughly equivalent to expecting only about three finds—so an origin up to 60 million years earlier remains plausible if early plants turned up several times less often, as happens during at least one other long sparse stretch in the fossil record. Because fossils quickly reach near-normal discovery rates by the late Silurian, vascular plants must either have originated shortly before their first known fossils or have existed for a long time in a rarely found state before rapidly becoming much easier to find.
