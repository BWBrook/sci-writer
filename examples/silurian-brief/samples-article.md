# Two sample passages for the F3 article

Fable wrote these on 16 November 2026 to show a possible voice for the article. They use only facts
already in the article's draft, and nobody has checked them against the ledger. They predate Barry's
critique of the storyline paragraph, so they have not been held to it.

## A possible opening

> The same DNA has given two ages for the ancestor of living land plants. Harris and colleagues
> dated it to 515–493 million years ago. Hooper and colleagues took the same sequences, revised the
> fossil calibrations, and obtained 457–450. The genomes were identical. What moved the answer by 55
> million years was a judgement about missing fossils: how long a lineage can exist before it leaves
> one that somebody finds.

Fable's own note: "The genomes were identical." is a short line placed for effect. One on a page is
acceptable. As a habit it would be the performed rhythm Barry warned against.

## The rule of three

> Doctors have a rule for reading an absence. If 300 patients take a drug and none reacts badly, the
> true rate of reactions could still be one in a hundred; the trial was too small to rule out three
> expected cases. Fossils work the same way. Vascular plants turn up at a certain rate after their
> oldest find. Had they been turning up at that rate earlier, the empty rock below could stretch
> only as far as three expected finds. With 48 finds in 33 million years, three finds take about 2
> million years, which puts the limit at 428.8 Ma. If those 48 are really 24 independent
> discoveries, three finds take twice as long, and the limit lands on the published maximum of 431.2
> Ma.

Held to the critique, this passage has faults: "reading an absence" is the metaphor Barry rejected,
and "rate", "vascular plants" and "Ma" appear without definition. The version in `draft-1.md`
repairs them.
